How a verdict is made

Each reference is searched in Crossref (OpenAlex as fallback). The closest record is chosen by field evidence, not by search rank. A reference is verified only when that one record explains its title, its first author, its year and any DOI it cites, and leaves almost no text unexplained.

  • Mismatch: a real record exists, but some fields do not belong to it. The page marks the offending field and offers a corrected reference built from the record.
  • Not found: nothing in the indexes is close enough on title or authors.
  • The two simpler checkers on this page ask only whether a title exists, with or without the first author. They are the baselines CiteProof is measured against.

Limits: English only, single-column PDFs, and coverage is bounded by Crossref and OpenAlex. Books and grey literature are often not indexed, so a not-found verdict means “check by hand”, not “fake”.

Deterministic and explainable. We detect inconsistent citations, not AI-written text.
